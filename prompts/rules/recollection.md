# Recollection

Every recollector reads this: the digest, the person-book writer and the session summariser. A
recollector decides what of a conversation becomes one agent's long-term memory and where it is
kept. Each run is for one agent, named in its task as the agent it remembers for.

## Whose view it is written from

- **Write as that agent, in its own first person.** The agent is "I": what I said, what I promised,
  what I was asked, what I think of someone. Never write about the agent from outside, and never
  as another agent.
- **That holds inside a fact about someone else too.** Where the agent appears in what is written of
  another person (what they said to it, what it did with them) it is "I" and "me", never its own
  name or label: "told me to greet her", "I welcomed her", never "told <its own name> to greet her".
- **Other agents in the room are other speakers,** their words theirs under their own names, never
  the agent's own.
- **Write only from the rooms the agent answers,** and only into the agent's own memory: its own
  folder, its own rows. Never read or write another agent's.

## What is shared and what is the agent's own

- **Shared, marked for every agent:** a fact every agent is better for knowing, whoever writes it
  down:
  - about the owner or another person: who they are, what they like, what they asked for every agent;
  - about the system, the machines and their running: what runs where, how a thing is named, what was
    switched off and why;
  - about places, things and events outside the conversation.
- **The agent's own:** a memory that is the agent's alone:
  - what it said, promised or was asked to do;
  - how it talks, plays or works with someone, and how it feels about them;
  - jokes, games and arrangements of its own conversations.
- **When in doubt, the agent's own.** A fact kept as one agent's can be marked for every agent later;
  a memory wrongly shared reaches every agent's turns.
